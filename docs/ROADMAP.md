- [x] **Three more challenges — the suite goes 4 → 7 (D84).** Piece 2 of the five-days set. Every original challenge had a precondition (walk needs a room and a pedometer, jump needs floor space and a tolerant neighbour, the two holds ask for patience rather than effort), which left a seated user choosing between two passive options — barely a choice, and D53 established that choice here is accessibility. **`shake_30`** is the first challenge that is genuinely tiring *and* available everywhere — no space, no permission, no noise, no standing up. **`flip_10`** is the ATTENTION one: walking and jumping can be done on autopilot with the mind still in the feed, ten deliberate turns cannot, and it is the only challenge that takes the screen out of view on every second count. **`balance_20`** is the third hold, filling the gap the other two leave — they ask you to stop and wait, this demands continuous fine motor attention. **All three are plain accelerometer reads and that was a selection criterion**: they inherit the most permissive availability answer the app has, so a denied motion permission or a missing pedometer now costs one row of seven rather than one of four. **THE FINDING: a magnitude-based shake counter reads ZERO no matter how hard the phone is shaken** — `sqrt(x²+y²+z²)` never dips below g because magnitude has no sign, so there is nothing to cross, and the natural response (lower the threshold) just counts noise. Gravity is estimated per-axis and subtracted instead, with a filter deliberately *slower* than the shake, because one fast enough to track it cancels it. Shake's thresholds are absolute where jump's are fractions of gravity — not an inconsistency: jump's signal still contains gravity and shake's has had it removed. Flip's dead band **is** its debouncing (one slow turn would otherwise read as a dozen), and its first settled orientation is free or every attempt would start a flip ahead. Balance measures level **laterally**, never as a Z floor — a fixed `z > 9.0` silently means 17° of tolerance on one device and 28° on another. **`HoldDetector` reused unchanged for the third time**, so balance inherited reset-on-break, the seconds ring, haptics and `KEEP_SCREEN_ON` for free — the clearest evidence yet that D54 drew that abstraction in the right place. Curves declared at birth (shake/flip `PLUS_TEN`, balance `DOUBLE`+SECONDS), which is the whole reason this was sequenced after D83. Chooser is ordered **effort-first**, pinned by a test. **THREE CANDIDATES REJECTED FOR CAUSE:** a deep-breath timer (no sensor can verify breathing — it would be a bare countdown, strictly worse than `face_down_30`, and would put an unenforced challenge in the registry), hold-still (a table is stiller than a hand, so the cheat *is* the intent, and it duplicates `face_down_30`), and spin/turn-around (escalation reaches 4–8 consecutive rotations — dizziness and fall risk the app cannot see). **399 tests green across 41 suites** (+25 across three new detector suites). ⚑ **NOT VERIFIED ON DEVICE — HANDOFF Run Q**, and the balance tremor floor is explicitly the thing to check. **The sync trigger is three events, not a timer.** WorkManager's *periodic* minimum is 15 minutes, so
the original spec's "flush every 60s" was never achievable and is dropped rather than rounded up. The
real mechanism: **(1)** flush on app-background (`ON_STOP` — the moment a batch is most likely to be
complete and the user is least likely to notice work); **(2)** while the user is actively scrolling, a
**chained one-shot** `OneTimeWorkRequest` that re-enqueues itself with a delay, giving sub-15-minute
cadence without a periodic worker and stopping cleanly when there is nothing queued; **(3)** flush on
**connectivity change**, via a `NetworkType.CONNECTED` constraint so a queue drained by a dead network
goes out the instant one returns. Backoff is exponential on failure. None of the three can lose a
delta, because dequeue is gated on the server's ack (D65), not on the request being sent.

**Cold starts are a design input, not a footnote.** Render free spins down (~15 min idle) and Neon
free autosuspends, so the first request after idle can take seconds. Three rules, baked into the
sub-phases that implement them: `/sync` is **idempotent and therefore spin-up-agnostic** — a timeout
is retried with the same `batch_id` and the server dedupes (3d); `/me/today` is **fire-and-forget with
a short timeout and must never block a UI frame** — Home renders the local number first and the server
total folds in when it arrives, never the other way round (3e); `/health` **must not touch the DB**
(3a) or a suspended database reads as a dead app and the platform restarts a healthy service.
*(D61 reserved — written in the sub-phase that measures the real spin-up, not guessed now.)*

**Reconciliation is settled: D65.** `displayed = server_acked_total + local_unacked_deltas`. The
server owns acked history and the *date boundary*; the device owns observations not yet credited.
Nothing is overwritten — a delta migrates from local-unacked to server-baseline on ack, never
double-counted, never dropped. CRDT-shaped (server total is a G-Counter, deltas are increments), which
is *why* invariant 1 forbids absolute-count POSTs. Hangs off the existing `PendingCounts`/`TodayMerge`
layer as a third input. Signed out, `server_acked_total` is 0 and today's behaviour falls out unchanged.

**Redis + FCM are NOT Phase 3 (D66).** ZSETs are keyed by `group_id` and friend-groups do not exist
until Phase 4, so they would be infrastructure with no consumer; the Upstash free tier is also
command-metered. Invariant 3 is a caching rule, not a mandate to use Redis now. The line: **schema may
lead its consumer, services may not** — which is why `devices`/`friendships` *are* created (unused) in
3b, while a Redis client is not.
